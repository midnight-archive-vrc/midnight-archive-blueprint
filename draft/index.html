How everything gets shelved working notes · v0.4.1

This draft shows one way to sort two kinds of things into one archive: Wikipedia articles, and full public-domain books from Project Gutenberg. Everything here is a proposal unless it is tagged Settled. The main blueprint, with the concept art, is here.

The shelves are on the towers' outside walls and face the sky, with a railed walkway around every level and a stair winding up the outside. Settled Articles become slim volumes on a floor's lower shelves. Books become thick volumes on the shelves just above them. Both use one subject map, so a topic's books stand next to its articles, and links run between them.

The inside of each tower holds no books. It is cozy space for lying around and talking, themed by the tower's topic. Settled What goes in is proposed below.

Books: Project Gutenberg has about 75,000 public-domain books. Its catalog gives each one a Library of Congress class, subject headings, an author and a language. Nothing is downloaded yet. Proposal

Articles: the ~500,000 most-read Wikipedia articles. Settled The test world starts with the ~50k Vital Articles.

Every drawing and count on this page is for ~500,000 articles, and every count is an estimate.

C-101

Shelving rules

Proposal

An article slim volume · lower shelves

  1. 1 · SourceWikipedia: the ~500,000 most-read articles. The test world starts with the ~50k Vital Articles.
  2. 2 · TowerIts Vital Articles top topic. Articles outside that list: a topic model picks the tower.
  3. 3 · FloorIts Vital Articles sub-list.
  4. 4 · ShelfThe lower shelves of that floor's outside wall.
  5. 5 · OrderThe curated list's own order first, then the rest by popularity, most-read first.

Links run between them. An article about a book or an author gets a "read the book" link. A book links to its author's article and to subject articles. Articles keep linking articles.

A book thick volume · upper shelves

  1. 1 · SourceProject Gutenberg: full public-domain text, with its catalog record.
  2. 2 · TowerIts Library of Congress class, through the table below. P (literature) goes to the Literature tower.
  3. 3 · FloorLiterature: its language or form. Other towers: the closest sub-list.
  4. 4 · ShelfThe shelves above that floor's articles.
  5. 5 · OrderLibrary of Congress call number, then author.

Low and high. On every floor the most-read things sit lowest, on the walkway where you arrive. Deep cuts climb higher, a walkway or two up the outside stair.

Floors and order Proposal

  • Floors are sub-topics. Articles use the Vital Articles sub-lists, as in v0.3.
  • Literature floors: English, American, French, German, Classical (Greek and Latin), Other languages, Poetry, Drama, Children's. A book goes to its language floor unless it is poetry, drama or for children.
  • Literature is a 12th tower, split out of Arts. Most Gutenberg books are fiction, poetry and drama; in Arts they would swamp music and painting. It is numbered 12 so the v0.3 numbers stay put.
  • One shelf wall, facing out. Each floor's outside wall carries both: slim volumes (the encyclopedia articles) on the lower shelves, thick books (the full texts) above them. A railed walkway runs around every level, and a stair winds up the outside.
  • Order within a floor. Articles in the curated list's order, then by popularity. Books by call number, then author.
  • Low and high. The most-read sit lowest, on the walkway where you arrive. Deep cuts climb higher. On A-201 a dashed line in the articles marks where the curated list ends and the most-read tail begins.
  • Size estimate. A shelf bay holds about 100 slim volumes or 40 thick books, and the wall has 48 bays per level, so a level holds about 4,800 articles or 1,920 books. A floor gets as many levels as its articles and books need together, at least one.

Inside the towers Settled cozy, themed by topic

With the books on the outside, every tower's inside is free for lying around, cuddling and talking.

In every tower Proposal

  • Window seats built into the back of the shelf wall, each big enough for two. The backs of the books glow through small gaps like stained glass.
  • Stairs crossing the open middle between floors, meeting at landings piled with pillows.
  • Hammock nets strung across the middle every few floors, to lie on together and look up through the tower.
  • A slide spiralling down the middle for the quick way down, and a page updraft: step into a column of drifting glowing pages and float slowly up.
  • A pillow nest on the top floor, under glass, with the moon overhead.

Cross-links Proposal

  • An article about a book or an author gets a "Read the book" link.
  • A book links to its author's article, and to subject articles from its Library of Congress subject headings.
  • Articles keep linking articles, as in v0.3.
  • Every jump flies the item to you and adds to your glowing trail.

Wayfinding Proposal

  • Tower signs with the topic name, seen from far away. Tower size already tells you how much is inside.
  • Floor plaques at each floor's balcony.
  • Search by title or author, for articles and books alike.
  • A random door that opens a random book, for when you don't know where to start.
  • The glowing trail showing where you have been.

Storage budget choices, not decided

Text
Text that every visitor can read must sit on a trusted host. GitHub Pages is the trusted host that can hold this much. Each Pages site holds 1 GB. A few sites in the org are fine; dozens are not.
Articles
About 2 KB per article lead, so ~500,000 articles ≈ 1 GB, a whole site. Longer article text costs more.
Books
A full Gutenberg book is very roughly 300 to 400 KB of text. All 75,000 come to about 25 to 30 GB. That does not fit.
Pictures
Only for visitors with "Allow Untrusted URLs" on. Settled

Article textSearch and link tablesComplete booksOpening chaptersEach box is one 1 GB GitHub Pages site.

Assumptions: 350 KB per complete book (middle of 300 to 400); an opening chapter is the first ~20 KB; search and link tables ~0.1 GB, ~0.2 GB at 500k (a guess). Boxes are packed full; real sites need some headroom. Each site also has a soft limit of 100 GB of traffic a month, and one complete book is ~350 KB per open.

Settled so far Settled

  • A rabbit-hole archive of readable Wikipedia articles, still called The Midnight Archive.
  • About 500,000 most-read Wikipedia articles. The test world starts with the ~50k Vital Articles.
  • Pictures only for visitors with "Allow Untrusted URLs" on.
  • The books go on the towers' outside walls, facing out, as in the first concept art. The insides are cozy space for lying around and talking, each themed by its tower's topic.
  • Credits through one QR code and a hosted page. PCVR is the beauty target; Quest gets the detail baked into textures.

Open questions

  • Complete books: option A, option B, or something else?
  • Is a 12th tower for Literature right, or should books share the Arts tower?
  • Literature floors: by language first, or by form (poetry, drama) first?
  • Where do books with no LoC class go? And general works (A) and military and naval science (U, V)?
  • English-language books only, or every language Gutenberg has?
  • Read Project Gutenberg's terms on reusing its files and its name before hosting copies.
  • Which cozy pieces go in every tower: window seats, crossing stairs, hammock nets, the slide, the page updraft, the pillow nest?
  • The seven themes marked New (People, History, Arts, Society, Technology, Mathematics, Literature): keep, change or swap?

Not changed by these notes: narration and the NPCs. See the main blueprint.
