How everything gets shelved draft v0.4 · not the live plan

This draft shows one way to sort two kinds of things into one archive: Wikipedia articles, and full public-domain books from Project Gutenberg. Everything here is a proposal unless it is tagged Decided. The live plan is v0.3.

Articles become slim volumes on the inner ring of a floor. Books become thick volumes on the outer ring of the same floor. Both use one subject map, so a topic's books stand next to its articles, and links run between them.

Books: Project Gutenberg has about 75,000 public-domain books. Its catalog gives each one a Library of Congress class, subject headings, an author and a language. Nothing is downloaded yet. Proposed

C-101

Shelving rules

Proposed

An article slim volume · inner ring

  1. 1 · SourceWikipedia. v1: Vital Articles, Level 5. Later: the most-read articles.
  2. 2 · TowerIts Vital Articles top topic. Articles outside that list: a topic model picks the tower.
  3. 3 · FloorIts Vital Articles sub-list.
  4. 4 · ShelfInner ring of that floor.
  5. 5 · OrderThe curated list's own order first, then the rest by popularity, most-read first.

Links run between the rings. An article about a book or an author gets a "read the book" link. A book links to its author's article and to subject articles. Articles keep linking articles.

A book thick volume · outer ring

  1. 1 · SourceProject Gutenberg: full public-domain text, with its catalog record.
  2. 2 · TowerIts Library of Congress class, through the table below. P (literature) goes to the Literature tower.
  3. 3 · FloorLiterature: its language or form. Other towers: the closest sub-list.
  4. 4 · ShelfOuter ring of that floor.
  5. 5 · OrderLibrary of Congress call number, then author.

Low and high. On every floor the most-read things sit lowest, near the balcony where you arrive. Deep cuts climb higher.

Floors and order Proposed

  • Floors are sub-topics. Articles use the Vital Articles sub-lists, as in v0.3.
  • Literature floors: English, American, French, German, Classical (Greek and Latin), Other languages, Poetry, Drama, Children's. A book goes to its language floor unless it is poetry, drama or for children.
  • Literature is a 12th tower, split out of Arts. Most Gutenberg books are fiction, poetry and drama; in Arts they would swamp music and painting. It is numbered 12 so the v0.3 numbers stay put.
  • Two shelf rings on every floor. Inner ring: slim volumes, the encyclopedia articles. Outer ring: thick books, the full texts.
  • Order within a floor. Articles in the curated list's order, then by popularity. Books by call number, then author.
  • Low and high. The most-read sit lowest, near the floor's balcony. Deep cuts climb higher. On A-201 a dashed line in the inner ring marks where the curated list ends and the most-read tail begins.
  • Size estimate. A shelf bay holds about 100 slim volumes or 40 thick books, and a ring has 48 bays, so a level holds about 4,800 articles or 1,920 books. Each floor gets at least one level.

Cross-links Proposed

  • An article about a book or an author gets a "Read the book" link.
  • A book links to its author's article, and to subject articles from its Library of Congress subject headings.
  • Articles keep linking articles, as in v0.3.
  • Every jump flies the item to you and adds to your glowing trail.

Wayfinding Proposed

  • Tower signs with the topic name, seen from far away. Tower size already tells you how much is inside.
  • Floor plaques at each floor's balcony.
  • Search by title or author, for articles and books alike.
  • A random door that opens a random book, for when you don't know where to start.
  • The glowing trail showing where you have been.

Storage budget choices for Jane, not decided

Text
Text that every visitor can read must sit on a trusted host. GitHub Pages is the trusted host that can hold this much. Each Pages site holds 1 GB. A few sites in the org are fine; dozens are not.
Articles
About 2 KB per article lead: 50k ≈ 0.1 GB, 100k ≈ 0.2 GB, 500k ≈ 1 GB. Longer article text costs more (the v1 build estimates 100 to 300 MB for its 50k).
Books
A full Gutenberg book is very roughly 300 to 400 KB of text. All 75,000 come to about 25 to 30 GB. That does not fit.
Pictures
Only for visitors with "Allow Untrusted URLs" on, served from Cloudflare. Decided

Article textSearch and link tablesComplete booksOpening chaptersEach box is one 1 GB GitHub Pages site.

Assumptions: 350 KB per complete book (middle of 300 to 400); an opening chapter is the first ~20 KB; search and link tables ~0.1 GB, ~0.2 GB at 500k (a guess). Boxes are packed full; real sites need some headroom. Each site also has a soft limit of 100 GB of traffic a month, and one complete book is ~350 KB per open.

  • A scale-up beyond the v1 list. Jane approved about 500,000 most-read articles ("works for me"); her latest message says 100,000. Which number is still open.
