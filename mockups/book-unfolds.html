The Midnight Archive · reading idea 6

The book unfolds into the air

Open a long book and its sections fly out as parchment leaves that hang in a gentle arc around you. Pull one in to read it, then fold them all back into the book. Short articles just open as a normal book.

In your hands:

The book and page text here was written for this mock-up, not taken from Wikipedia. Only Octopus and Cuttlefish are real books in this demo; other gold words say where they would lead.

In VRChat

  • VR: open the book and the leaves spread around you at reading distance. Reach out and grab a leaf to pull it close; let go and it drifts back into the arc. Closing the book folds them all back in.
  • Desktop: look around with the mouse and click a leaf to bring it in front of you. Arrow keys step to the next section; click away to let it go.
  • Quest: each leaf is a simple quad, and the text for all of them is drawn on one shared canvas texture, with at most ~7 leaves. Articles with more sections merge the small ones.
  • Where: best for long articles, at a lectern or in a nook. Not while flying: the leaves stay where you opened the book, and moving away folds them back.
  • Short articles (2 or 3 sections) skip the leaves and open as a normal two-page book.

Builds on

  • Clickable gold words: the same overlay hit areas sit over the words on each leaf.
  • A− / A+ with re-paging: each leaf re-pages on its own; a long section gets page turns on its leaf.
  • Back history: Back folds the leaves away and brings the previous book.
  • Grabbable books off a lit bay (bays light when you stand next to them, never by facing them): unchanged. You carry the book to a lectern or nook to unfold it.
  • Others see the book you hold (title on the cover), open or closed.
  • Books from GitHub Pages: the ~5 s load hides behind carrying the book and the opening animation; the leaves only fly once the text has arrived.

Open questions

  1. Leaves only for long articles, or always available (even a two-section book could unfold)?
  2. An arc around you, or a smaller fan above the open book?
  3. Should other players see your leaves (and read along), or only you?
